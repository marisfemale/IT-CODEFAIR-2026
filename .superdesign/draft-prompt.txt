Design a polished desktop analytical dashboard called "ConnectNT Investment Lens". It is a decision-support layer on top of the Australian Government First Nations Connectivity Mapping Tool, not a replacement map and not Australian Government branded. The primary users are infrastructure investors, funding bodies, NT Government planners, telecommunications teams, and community-sector decision makers.

Create one dense but calm 1600px desktop screen with excellent information hierarchy. Use the design-system context exactly.

Header: ConnectNT Investment Lens title; subtitle "Investment screening for remote connectivity"; small chips "NT • 792 communities" and "Data vintage: 2021–2025"; actions "Methodology", "Open official map", and solid "Export shortlist". Include a persistent slim disclaimer beneath the header: "Screening result only — validate service, feasibility, cost and community priorities before investment."

Left filter rail (about 260px): search community; multiselect NT Government Region with Greater Darwin / Top End / East Arnhem / Big Rivers / Barkly / Central Australia; Community Profile with Major, Minor, Family Outstation, Town Camp, Village, Town; Population with Available / Unknown and bands; Mapped Connectivity with No mobile, No 4G, No 5G, No terrestrial NBN; Priority Tier High / Medium / Low; Evidence Confidence High / Medium / Low. Show active filter chips and a Reset action. Filters must look usable, not decorative.

Main workspace: first a four-cell KPI strip showing 792 communities screened; 442 with no mapped mobile plus no mapped terrestrial NBN; 324 with population unknown; 10 current shortlist. Each KPI has context and an understated data-quality note.

Below, a two-column analysis area. On the left, a large NT-only "Investment screening layer" map card. Render a simplified Northern Territory outline with community point symbols: high = red diamond, medium = amber square, low = green circle, evidence incomplete = slate outlined triangle. Include map/table toggle, zoom controls, a clear legend, and a small note that points represent modelled/public-source screening. Do not create a national coverage explorer or multiple raw-data layer controls.

On the right, a "Top 10 screening candidates" panel linked to active filters. It must disclose "Sorted by connectivity need; population breaks ties when known." Show ranked rows with community name, region, community type, priority badge, evidence-confidence badge, and two compact reason chips. Use respectful example names from the data such as Walytjatjata, Murun Murula, Kurkutjara, Jiwaranpa, Ukatjupu, Wangalinji, Mamadi, Duck Ponds, Mirridi, and Batton Hill. Include a visible "Why?" affordance on every row.

Below the map, include a need-versus-confidence 2x2 matrix with four labelled quadrants: "Shortlist for due diligence" (high need/high confidence), "Validate first" (high need/low confidence), "Monitor" (lower need/high confidence), and "Data gap" (lower need/low confidence). Use count badges and a selected point. This matrix is critical and must be easy to understand.

At the bottom or as an open right-side detail drawer, show the selected community evidence panel for Batton Hill as a clearly labelled prototype example. Include: High screening priority; Medium evidence confidence; community type Family Outstation; population 2 with source status; no mapped mobile; no mapped terrestrial NBN; 141 km nearest recorded mobile site; 215 km nearest recorded Wi-Fi. Show a transparent factor breakdown, raw values, data source/vintage labels, "What this means", "What remains unknown" (satellite, lived reliability, cost, power/backhaul, community preference), and next step "Due diligence and community verification". Exact coordinates and infrastructure IDs must not appear.

Use flat technical-minimalist styling, no gradients, no large shadows, no decorative photos, and no government crest. Priority must be encoded by color plus shape plus text. Make the dashboard credible enough for a judging presentation but honest about uncertainty. Use only the fonts, colors, spacing, and component styles defined in the design system. Do not introduce any fonts, colors, or visual styles not in the design system.
